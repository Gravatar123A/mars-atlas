# Limits and unknowns

MARS//ATLAS is an independent research and educational prototype. It is **not** an official NASA operational tool, an EVA planner, or a certified navigation system.

The region selector includes a global MOLA map and globe, Jezero and Gale terrain routes, and a Gusev imagery study. The global MOLA layer is rendered color hillshade, so the globe does not provide numerical planet-wide elevation or slope. Gale uses a 6 × 6 km extract at 20 m sample spacing from a merged USGS source whose underlying HiRISE, CTX, HRSC, and MOLA coverage varies. A 1 m mosaic output pixel does not imply 1 m effective resolution or validated vertical accuracy everywhere. Its Bradbury start is cited; the nearby east destination is a scenario point. Gusev has no packaged numeric route DEM, so elevation, slope, route, and local 3D terrain functions are unavailable there. Live imagery is not a substitute for DEM data.

- **Terrain scale:** Routes use 20 m CTX DEM cells in the landing footprint, or an 80 m sampled CTX DEM beyond it. They cannot resolve rocks, sand ripples, weak soil, cliff edges within cells, or other small-scale hazards. The slope threshold and gradient bands are scenario controls, not validated astronaut safety thresholds.
- **3D visualization:** The landing surface samples the route DEM at 320 m intervals. The broader Jezero surface is derived from a separate native 20 m CTX DEM, packaged at 80 m and rendered at about 1 km visual spacing. Missing source cells remain missing. Heights are shown at 6× scale. Colors are a visual elevation palette; the surface is not a photograph or a higher-resolution survey.
- **Global globe:** The globe maps a composite of 32 NASA Mars Trek MOLA color-hillshade tiles to a sphere. It is an orthographic visual rendering, not a 3D numeric elevation model. Zooming the globe does not reveal more scientific resolution than the source texture contains. Local 3D DEM meshes are separate views.
- **Rover history:** Perseverance and Curiosity coordinate paths are snapshots of public JPL feeds, simplified for browser display. The app does not claim to update them live or interpret them as safe walking routes. Spirit and Opportunity paths are linked to NASA maps but not traced as precise coordinate lines. The Opportunity global landing marker is approximate.
- **Study area:** Routing uses the 31.9 × 30.2 km landing-area CTX DEM footprint and its 2,413,152 cells when possible. A second 89 × 101 km Jezero CTX DEM extends exploratory routing at 80 m sample spacing. The 2D map outlines both rectangular package footprints; some regional cells may still be missing. A route requires one DEM to cover every stop. The Mars Trek imagery may extend beyond either DEM.
- **Vertical uncertainty:** The DEM product name describes MOLA topography and a delta geoid adjustment, but this prototype has not independently quantified absolute vertical accuracy or stereo-model uncertainty. Elevation and route metrics are therefore exploratory.
- **Path model:** Eight-neighbor A* prevents blocked diagonal corners but does not account for turning radius, exact traverseable surfaces, line-of-sight, obstacles smaller than 20 m or 80 m respectively, or field-verified navigation. Regional downsampling may miss narrow hazards and can substantially change apparent slopes. It cannot certify any route.
- **Measurement tool:** The two-point readout is great-circle horizontal distance, not walkable distance. Endpoint elevations come from nearest stored DEM cells, and the app does not infer what lies between the two points. It suppresses elevation differences when points use different DEM products.
- **Mission operations:** Time is a simple distance/speed calculation. The budget is a hypothetical one-way threshold, not an EVA allowance. The app has no suit, life-support, radiation, communications, daylight, thermal, return-to-base, or scientific sampling duration model. It displays no live Mars weather.
- **Scientific sites:** The landing, Sol 426, and Sol 464 coordinates are cited historical locations. They are not outcrop boundaries or EVA destinations certified by NASA. Arbitrary user-selected points are model inputs, not verified scientific targets. The Jezero delta description is regional science context.
- **Map service:** Visual layers are live NASA Mars Trek WMTS requests and can fail, load slowly, or have gaps. Numeric routing uses the checked-in source-derived grid and remains available if those tiles fail.
- **Saved plans:** Plans are stored only in the current browser's local storage. They are not synced, shared, or backed up. Routes are recalculated when a plan is loaded.
- **Data provenance:** The source registry identifies products and limits, but the individual stereo acquisition times, detailed error budget, exact redistribution conditions, and full release history require further review.
- **Competition status:** The full 2026 statement and resources were reviewed through November 15; see [CHALLENGE_COMPLIANCE.md](CHALLENGE_COMPLIANCE.md). The website deployment is distinct from a competition submission. This repository does not claim that a competition entry was submitted.

The next scientific improvements should verify the DEM's vertical datum and uncertainty, add a confidence mask, align a higher-resolution DTM for selected local areas, and compare route predictions to independent terrain measurements. The next product improvements should add documented science-site geometry and broader mobile usability checks.
